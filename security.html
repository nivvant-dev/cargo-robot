Security Policy

Last updated: October 24, 2026 · Website only

This website is a static page with no accounts, database or uploads. This policy describes the protections that are in place, the parts that depend on the hosting provider, and how to report a security concern responsibly.

Short version: No logins, no server-side data, no third-party scripts. The site is served over HTTPS with a restrictive Content Security Policy. No website is perfectly secure, and hosting infrastructure is outside our control. Report problems to the team rather than testing them on live users.

1. Scope of this policy

This policy covers the static website at this domain: its HTML, CSS, JavaScript, images and the locally stored trial log. The site has no user accounts, no server-side application, no database, no payment flow and no file uploads. The only interactive data is saved in the visitor's own browser.

It does not cover the physical robot, its firmware, electrical system or test procedures. Those are covered by the project safety guidance and the Terms of Use.

2. Protections in place

Security measures and their purpose
Measure What it does
HTTPS The site is served over an encrypted connection by the hosting provider. Mixed insecure content is not used.
Content Security Policy A restrictive policy limits scripts, styles, images and fonts to the site itself, blocks framing and plugins, disables base-uri overrides and disallows outbound connections. This reduces the impact of an injected script.
No third-party code No analytics, advertising, trackers, remote fonts, CDN scripts, embedded players or social widgets. Every script and style is a local file that can be reviewed.
No secrets in the site The project contains no API keys, passwords, tokens or private configuration. Nothing in the browser can be used to reach a backend, because there is no backend.
Safe rendering User-entered trial notes are inserted as text, not HTML, and bill-of-materials text is escaped. A note cannot become executable markup in another visitor's browser.
Local-only data Trials are stored with localStorage. They are not transmitted, and the site does not read data from other origins.
Privacy defaults No cookies, no account sessions, no cross-site tracking. The referrer is limited to the origin for cross-origin requests.
Host security headers A _headers file adds clickjacking, MIME-sniffing and referrer protections on hosts that support it (Netlify and Cloudflare Pages do).

These measures reduce risk; they do not make the site immune to every attack. A static site can still be affected by a compromised host, a compromised device, a browser vulnerability or a typo-squatted domain.

3. Hosting and limits of our control

The site is hosted by a third-party static hosting provider. The provider controls TLS, edge servers, DNS, access logs and the physical infrastructure. Its own security program and incident process apply. The team cannot guarantee the provider's availability, patching or logging, and is not responsible for outages or breaches originating in that infrastructure.

Standard security headers are supplied in _headers for hosts that read it. GitHub Pages does not apply custom response headers from that file, so on GitHub Pages the browser-enforced protections come from the CSP meta tag in each page and from GitHub's own platform defaults. If header-level protections are important to you, deploy to Netlify or Cloudflare Pages instead. No configuration can promise a perfect security score; scanner grades are not a guarantee.

4. Data and privacy

The site does not collect personal information on a server. Trial data stays in your browser and can be cleared with Clear all trials. Emails you choose to send go to a school or mentor mailbox, not to a site database.

For the full picture, including hosting logs, see the Privacy Policy. Do not put passwords, private keys or other sensitive information into trial notes or into an email to the team.

5. Reporting a security problem

If you believe you have found a security issue, please email berryda@cfbisd.edu with the subject “Security”. Include:

  • the page address or file involved;
  • a short description of the issue and its possible impact;
  • steps to reproduce it, with screenshots or a minimal example if useful;
  • how we can contact you for follow-up.

Please act in good faith: do not access, change or delete other people's data, do not disrupt the site or host, and do not use social engineering against the team, school or provider. Give us a reasonable chance to investigate and fix the issue before publishing details.

This is a student project and we do not offer a bug bounty or payment. We will acknowledge reports when we can during the school term, may ask the school or hosting provider for help, and will credit reporters if they would like. The team's intention is not to pursue action against research conducted responsibly under this policy; this does not override the school's or host's own rights or legal obligations.

6. Out of scope

The following are not vulnerabilities in this site:

  • the theoretical compromise of the hosting provider, DNS or a visitor's device;
  • missing headers or settings that the chosen host does not support;
  • automated scanner findings without a demonstrated impact;
  • spam, phishing or social-engineering messages that do not involve the site;
  • the absence of a login, database or payment system (there is intentionally none);
  • issues in the physical robot or its firmware, which are outside this website policy.

If you are unsure whether something is in scope, ask first instead of testing it.

7. The physical robot is a separate safety matter

This policy covers the website only. The prototype includes motors, a lifting mechanism, sharp edges and electrical power. Follow the project's safety section, use an emergency stop, keep hands clear of moving parts, and have a teacher or mentor supervise powered tests. See the safety disclaimer.

8. Updates to this policy

We may revise this policy as the site or its hosting changes. The date at the top of the page is the current version. Material changes will be described here rather than silently replacing the page.
